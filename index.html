      Incident flow and who does what

      1. DetectionMonitoring alert or client report. Ticket created immediately.
      2. TriageSeverity set with the tech lead; business impact confirmed with the client.
      3. CommunicationFirst message to the client within the response target, then at the agreed frequency.
      4. ResolutionFix or workaround by the engineering team. The project manager tracks the deadline and prepares the closure message.
      5. Post-incident reviewFor P1 and P2: causes, preventive actions, owners and due dates, logged in the RAID log.

      How each metric is computed

      • Deployment frequency: production deployments per week, corrective ones included.
      • Lead time for changes: from first commit to running in production. The median is used, not the mean, because a few slow changes would distort it.
      • Change failure rate: deployments that needed a rollback, a hotfix or an incident fix, divided by all deployments.
      • Failed deployment recovery time: from the failing deployment to the corrective deployment. Median over failed deployments.
      • Deployment rework rate: unplanned corrective deployments divided by all deployments. Newer metric, no established benchmark.

      Data sources in practice: deployment timestamps from the CI/CD tool, first-commit time from the repository, failure flag from rollbacks or incident tickets. The tech lead owns this tooling.

      These metrics come from tooling chosen by the tech lead. The project manager tracks them and links them to delivery commitments, without deciding the engineering practices that move them. Use them to spot friction, not to rank people or set targets: a target turns a metric into a game.

      Guardrails

      • Anonymise before pasting. Replace client, people and product names with placeholders if the tool is not approved for confidential data.
      • AI drafts, people decide. Read every output before it goes to a client or a steering committee.
      • Check numbers and dates against the trackers. AI can misread or invent them.
      • Technical choices stay with the tech lead. Use AI to prepare questions, not verdicts.
      • Be transparent. Follow your organisation's policy on disclosing AI-assisted documents.
